  title: "Why Gutters Are the First Upgrade We Recommend in Southwest Florida",
  paraThree: "A partial gutter run is one of the most consistent findings we document on newly built homes from Port Charlotte to Cape Coral. The front elevation gets gutters because it is the elevation buyers photograph, and the rest of the roof drains straight onto the ground. In a drier climate that is a minor issue. Here it is not. Southwest Florida takes well over 50 inches of rain a year, most of it in concentrated summer downpours, and an unguttered roof line concentrates all of that water into a narrow strip of soil a few inches from the slab. Over time that shows up as eroded mulch beds and washed out grade, soil that stays saturated against the foundation, splash staining and premature wear on stucco and paint, water finding its way under door thresholds and lanai slabs, and settlement cracking where the soil under the edge of the slab repeatedly saturates and dries. Adding gutters to the remaining elevations while the builder is still on site costs a fraction of what it costs to hire a contractor later, and nothing close to what regrading or foundation repair costs.",
  titleTwo: "The Bigger Lesson: Buy What You Cannot Easily Add Later",

Paint, cabinet hardware, mirrors, light fixtures, and some flooring landed on the wait side, because you can change any of them on a weekend without opening a wall. Drainage belongs firmly in the first category.
